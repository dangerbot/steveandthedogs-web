The Vision

Everything fan. One system.

Most teams run a different vendor for every part of the fan journey. A website here, ticketing there, a separate app, CRM, merch, marketing, game-day tools. The fan feels the seams. The data never connects. Revenue leaks through the gaps.

Envorso Sports is one platform for all of it. Eight modules. One fan profile. Zero integrations.

The Principle

We do what we do best, so the team can do what they do best.

A team's job is to win. Build the roster, coach the squad, take the field. That is where their energy belongs.

Our job is the other side of game day. Fill the seats. Deepen the relationship. Turn a casual attendee into a fan who brings a friend.

That is the partnership. We do not sell software and walk away. We earn a share of the revenue we help create. When the team wins on the field and we fill the stands, everyone wins. When we do not deliver, we do not get paid.

The team builds the team. We build the fan.

The Strategy

Optimize for revenue per game.

Revenue follows two things working together. An experience worth coming back for, and pricing that turns loyalty into dollars without breaking trust.

They compound. Better experience earns pricing power. Pricing funds better features. Better features build happier fans. That flywheel is the product.

Experience→ Pricing power→ Better features→ Happier fans

And back to the top. Every game teaches the system more.

Effortless ticket purchase

Easy on the surface, smart underneath. Behavioral-economics pricing that works the seat map like an airline.

AI-personalized engagement

The right pick, the right player, the right merch, fan by fan, sharper every game.

The Headline Capability

Make Your Picks.

Every ticket comes with a pick. One tap, before the game. Delivered by text, because every fan has it and no one has to download a thing. Get it right, earn team credits. Credits buy tickets, concessions, merch.

For the fan, every game gets personal. Skin in the action beyond the scoreboard.

For the team, every pick requires a ticket, and every credit earned funnels back into team spend. Make Your Picks does not replace ticket sales. It grows them.

Risks designed against: friction (one tap is the whole experience) and gambling optics (no money in, credits redeem for team value only, the same shape as a punch card).

The Moat

The moat is the mix.

Every fan signal flows through one platform. Tickets, picks, credits, merch, attendance, and every nudge a fan responded to or ignored.

A standalone ticketing vendor sees tickets. A standalone app sees sessions. A standalone CRM sees campaigns. None of them sees the whole fan. We do.

The more games we run, the more we learn. The more we learn, the sharper the nudges. The platform compounds. A single-feature vendor cannot.

The Growth Unlock

Early growth is a friction problem.

A great first product is built. The next job is making it effortless for the next team to start.

Zero integrations already removes the integration tax. But every team still arrives with years of history living in disparate systems. Season-ticket records in one tool. Email lists in another. Merch history, CRM notes, ticketing exports, all somewhere else.

In my experience adopting new software, that is the hardest moment. Not the demo. The migration. How do I get everything I already have into the new system, fast, without a six-month project?

STEP 01

Ingest

AI-assisted import pulls a team's existing data and knowledge out of its scattered tools.

STEP 02

Seed

That history seeds the unified fan profile automatically. No blank slate, no manual rebuild.

STEP 03

Value on day one

The team feels the benefit immediately, not after a quarter of setup.

Get that right and landing team two, three, and ten stops being a sales problem and becomes a ramp. Friction down, growth up.

Where I Would Pressure-Test

What I would want to be wrong about.

Naming the soft spots is the point. Strong calls come from testing fast, not betting big.

The SMS engagement bet

It assumes the everyday fan taps a text pick. I would watch real fans try it before committing the whole loop to it.

The onboarding lift

Day-one migration is the unlock, but the real lift depends on how messy each team's existing data is. I would prototype against one real team's export first.

The pricing model

Revenue share aligns incentives and lowers the barrier to a yes. It is also a slower revenue curve. Worth pressure-testing with whoever owns commercial strategy.
